---
title: "Chasing Morning Light: Lessons from My Latest Oil Canvas"
date: "2026-09-05"
---

There is something magical about the studio around 7:00 AM. The sun hits the east-facing window just right, casting long, dramatic shadows across my easel and turning my messy palette into a mosaic of glowing color. Lately, I’ve been obsessed with trying to capture that exact quality of early light before it shifts into the harsh brightness of midday.

My latest piece, *First Light over Oak Creek*, started as a quick color study last Tuesday. I originally planned to keep it small—just a 6x8 panel—but the atmospheric mood was so addicting that I pulled out a fresh 16x20 stretched linen canvas the very next morning.

For this painting, I stuck to a pretty limited palette: French Ultramarine, Burnt Sienna, Yellow Ochre, and plenty of Titanium White. I find that when I restrict my colors, the lighting in the painting feels much more cohesive. The hardest part wasn't the composition or the values; it was resisting the urge to overwork the foreground shadows. With oil paint, it’s so easy to keep blending until you lose all the fresh, spontaneous energy of those initial brushstrokes.

I ended up using a heavy impasto technique for the highlight flickers on the grass, laying the paint down thick with a palette knife. That buttery texture of oil paint is really unmatched—it catches real light in the room, making the painting feel alive even when the studio lights are off.

I’m letting this one dry on the drying rack for the next week or two before deciding if it needs a final retouching glaze. In the meantime, I’d love to hear from you.
