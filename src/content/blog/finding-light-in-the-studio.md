There is something quiet and almost meditative about setting up in the studio early in the morning before the rest of the world wakes up. Lately, I’ve been spending a lot of time working on a new oil piece focused on capturing the soft, shifting light that spills through the trees during late afternoon.

Oil paint has a way of teaching you patience, whether you want to learn it or not. Unlike acrylics, which dry almost instantly, oils demand that you slow down and really observe. You have to work alongside the slow drying time, deciding when to blend wet-on-wet layers and when to step back for a day or two to let a section set before adding those sharp, final highlights.

In my most recent painting session, I found myself getting lost in the subtle shifts of color temperature. Playing cooler blues and muted purples off warm yellow ochres and deep earth tones always feels like a delicate balance. One brushstroke too many can easily turn a rich, glowing passage into a muddy gray, so knowing when to put the brush down is half the battle.

Lately, I’ve also been experimenting more with limited color palettes. Restricting my options forces me to rely much heavier on value and subtle mixing rather than reaching for a convenient tube of pre-mixed color. Mixing almost every shade from a few basic pigments on the glass palette feels deeply grounding. It connects me directly to the medium in a way that feels essential to the final mood of the piece.

I’m often asked how I know when a painting is finally finished. Honestly, it’s rarely a dramatic moment where everything suddenly clicks. More often, it’s just reaching a point where adding anything else would take away from the quiet story the canvas is already telling.

If you’ve been following along with my recent work, thank you so much for the support. I’m hoping to have this piece fully dry, varnished, and ready to share in detail over the coming weeks. Until then, I’ll be back at the easel starting on the next study.
